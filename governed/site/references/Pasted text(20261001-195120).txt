## 1. NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

1. **Originality (clean-room).** The market reference list in Section 3 is for gap analysis and positioning only.
   - Do not copy any provider's program titles, curriculum outlines, module names, descriptions, marketing copy, images, testimonials, salary claims, or faculty names.
   - Do not name or imply partnership with Johns Hopkins, MIT, UT Austin/McCombs, Great Learning, or any other institution.
   - Write every title, outcome and module originally.
2. **Honest credentials.** Use "Scholaris AI Academy Certificate of Completion" and "Professional Certificate" labels only. Never use "Postgraduate", "PG", "degree" or "accredited" unless an approval actually exists and is recorded.
3. **Honest career claims.** No salary, placement-rate or job-guarantee claims without verified, cited, current data. Describe career support as services offered, never outcomes guaranteed.
4. **Current and verified tooling.** Every framework, model and tool named must be checked as current at build time, with version pinned in lab environments. Flag anything deprecated.
5. **Responsible AI in every program.** Every program includes evaluation, guardrails, human-in-the-loop, privacy, security and bias content scaled to its audience.
6. **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.2 AA for all content. Captions/transcripts for all media. Alt text. Accessible notebooks and slides.
7. **Academic integrity.** The Scholaris AI Teaching Assistant explains and coaches but never completes graded work.

- Self-paced weekly modules
- Live weekend sessions (recorded and captioned)

### 3.1 Differentiation rules (avoid overlapping programs)
- **#1 vs #2 vs #3:**
  - #2 is the on-ramp: LLM apps, prompting, RAG, single agents.
  - #1 is production multi-agent systems: orchestration, memory, MCP/tool protocols, evaluation, observability, deployment, security.
  - #3 is the broadest: classical ML → deep learning → GenAI → agents. It is aimed at learners without an ML foundation.
- **#4** focuses on the software lifecycle: AI coding assistants, agentic coding workflows, test generation, code review agents, CI/CD agents, secure use in repos.
- **#5 vs #6:**
  - #5 includes no-code predictive ML (AutoML, classification/forecasting) plus agents.
  - #6 is GenAI-only: prompting, knowledge assistants, RAG via no-code tools, workflow automation.
- **#8 vs #9:**
  - #8 is for practitioners who implement agents in business processes (low-code).
  - #9 is for leaders who decide, fund, govern and scale AI: strategy, ROI, operating model, risk, change management.
- **#10 / #11** are domain programs with sector regulation and risk at the center.
  - #11 must stay focused on **operations, administration, documentation and population-level analytics**. It must not teach learners to make clinical diagnoses or treatment decisions with AI. Clinical AI content is limited to how such systems are evaluated, regulated and governed.

## 4. REQUIRED DESIGN PACKAGE (for EACH of the 11 programs)

1. **Catalog page:** title, one-paragraph description, who it's for, prerequisites, length, weekly hours, delivery mode, learning outcomes, tools used, capstone summary, credential, stackability, career services offered. Use honest claims only.
2. **Program learning outcomes:** 5 competencies written as measurable outcomes (Bloom's verbs), mapped to every module and assessment.
3. **Week-by-week curriculum map:** module title, topics, readings/resources, lab, activity, assessment, live session topic.
4. **Course shells in Scholaris** following the Scholaris course standard (Section 5).
5. **Hands-on In-Class Activities** for every module, in the Academy activity template (Section 6).
6. **Labs:**
   - Builder/Industry tracks: Student Starter notebook with TODOs plus Instructor Solution notebook (EXECUTED), runnable in Colab and the Scholaris Cloud Lab.
   - No-Code tracks: step-by-step tool walkthroughs with screenshots and a completed reference build.
7. **Datasets:** generated or properly licensed `.csv`/JSON datasets for every lab. Include a data card covering source, license, fields, intended use, and known biases. **No real personal, financial or patient data.**
8. **Mini Labs:** 2 per module (starter TODO plus EXECUTED solution).
9. **Assignments:** an enhanced assignment per course block with a detailed step-by-step walkthrough on a larger dataset.
10. **Quizzes and exams:** question banks with explanations; randomized pools.
11. **Projects and capstone:** brief, milestones, deliverables, rubric, sample exemplar, and a demo-day format.
12. **Rubrics:** analytic rubrics with criteria, rating bands and points, aligned to outcomes.
13. **Slides and recorded-lecture scripts** for each module (captioned).
14. **Instructor guide:** timing, facilitation notes, answer keys, common mistakes, live-session plans.
15. **Student edition** of every activity and worksheet without answers. **Instructor edition** with the answer key and an instructor-led sample prefilled.
16. **Credential definition:** completion criteria, badge metadata (skills, evidence, criteria URL), certificate template.
17. **Career-services hooks:** portfolio items produced, resume bullet suggestions, interview practice prompts, relevant job-role mapping (described as roles the skills apply to, not guarantees).
18. **Evaluation plan:** pre/post skills assessment, module feedback surveys, completion and mastery analytics.

## 5. SCHOLARIS COURSE STANDARD (apply per 10-week course block)

Programs **10 weeks or longer** are built from one or more 10-week course blocks. Each block contains:
- Full syllabus
- 5 competencies
- 10 topics/modules over a 10-week outline
- 5 assignments, 3 labs, 2 projects, 8 quizzes, midterm and final exams
- Automatic weekly announcements
- **Sequential module locking**

Scaling rules:
- **16–20 week programs** = 2 course blocks. The second block's final exam may be replaced by the capstone defense.
- **12–14 week programs** = 1 extended block, with the extra weeks used for the capstone.
- **The 6-week program (#7)** uses a scaled standard: 6 modules, 3 assignments, 2 labs, 1 project, 6 quizzes, a final practical assessment, weekly announcements, sequential locking.

Build every block using Scholaris Canvas-parity features:
- Modules with prerequisites and requirements
- Mastery Paths for remediation and enrichment
- Rubrics linked to outcomes
- Item banks
- Course Pacing for self-paced cohorts
- Discussion checkpoints
- Peer review on projects
- Learning Mastery Gradebook

## 7. CONTENT SPINE (cover across programs, depth scaled by track)

| Domain | Topics |
|---|---|
| Foundations | AI/ML basics, data, model types, LLM fundamentals, tokens/context, model selection, cost and latency |
| Prompting | Prompt design patterns, structured outputs, few-shot, system prompts, prompt evaluation |
| Retrieval | Embeddings, chunking, vector search, hybrid search, reranking, RAG evaluation, Agentic RAG |
| Agent design | Reasoning-and-acting loops, plan-and-execute, reflection/self-critique, tool use and function calling, memory (short/long-term), state machines/graphs |
| Multi-agent | Orchestrator–worker, hierarchical teams, debate/critique, handoffs, shared state, failure handling |
| Interoperability | Tool and context protocols (e.g., MCP), agent-to-agent communication, API integration |
| Frameworks (verify current versions) | LangGraph, LangChain, CrewAI, LlamaIndex, provider agent SDKs; no-code platforms for tracks #5–#7 |
| Engineering | Testing agents, evaluation datasets, LLM-as-judge with human calibration, observability/tracing, deployment, versioning, rollback |
| Security | Prompt injection, data exfiltration, tool permissioning, least privilege, sandboxing, secrets, supply-chain risk |
| Governance | Human-in-the-loop design, guardrails, auditability, bias and fairness, privacy (GDPR/CCPA/HIPAA/NDPA as relevant), AI regulation overview (e.g., EU AI Act), model risk management |
| Business value | Use-case selection, process mapping, ROI and total cost, build vs buy, change management, adoption metrics |
| Industry (#10) | Fraud/AML monitoring, credit risk support, financial reporting and reconciliation agents, research assistants, model risk management and audit trails. **No personalized investment advice agents.** |
| Industry (#11) | Revenue cycle, scheduling, prior-auth paperwork, documentation support, supply chain, population analytics, and evaluating/governing clinical AI. **No diagnostic or treatment-decision agents built by learners.** |

## 10. ASSESSMENT & CREDENTIAL RULES

**Completion requires:**
- At least 70% overall
- All labs submitted
- Capstone passed against its rubric
- At least 80% attendance or participation in live/mentored components (or an approved async alternative)

**Other rules:**
- **Mastery:** outcome mastery tracked per competency. Learners below mastery get Mastery Path remediation before the capstone.
- **Integrity:** AI-use policy per assignment (allowed / allowed with disclosure / not allowed). Capstone defense is live or recorded.
- **Credentials** carry the program name, competencies, capstone evidence link, issue date and verification URL. No institution names other than the Scholaris AI Academy (and the offering tenant, if approved).
